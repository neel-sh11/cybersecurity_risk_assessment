2. Purpose

This document describes the methodology, assessment principles, risk evaluation approach, and reference materials used throughout the GRC Risk Assessment Project.

The objective is to maintain consistency, transparency, and traceability across the assessment documents.

⸻

3. Assessment Methodology

The project follows a structured information security risk assessment approach.

Step 1: Define the Assessment Scope

Identify the organizational context, information assets, systems, and processes included in the assessment.

Step 2: Asset Identification

Identify and document relevant organizational assets, including:

* Hardware and computing devices.
* Software and applications.
* Business information and databases.
* Network infrastructure.
* User accounts and credentials.
* Supporting business processes.

Step 3: Threat and Vulnerability Identification

Identify potential threats and weaknesses that could affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of organizational assets.

Step 4: Risk Analysis

Evaluate identified risks using two primary factors:

* Likelihood: The probability of a risk event occurring.
* Impact: The potential consequences if the event occurs.

Step 5: Risk Evaluation

Calculate risk scores and classify risks according to the defined risk rating criteria.

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

Step 6: Risk Treatment

Determine appropriate treatment measures for identified risks.

Treatment options include:

* Risk Modification.
* Risk Avoidance.
* Risk Sharing.
* Risk Retention.

Step 7: Control Applicability

Review relevant ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls and document their applicability and justification in the Statement of Applicability (SoA).

Step 8: Implementation Tracking

Track proposed security controls, implementation responsibilities, progress, and supporting evidence.

Step 9: Residual Risk Assessment

Reassess risks after considering the effectiveness of implemented controls.

Step 10: Documentation and Review

Maintain assessment records, document decisions, and identify areas requiring further improvement.

4. Risk Scoring Methodology

The project uses a 5 × 5 qualitative risk assessment matrix.

Likelihood Scale

* 1 — Rare: Very unlikely to occur.
* 2 — Unlikely: Could occur under unusual circumstances.
* 3 — Possible: May occur occasionally.
* 4 — Likely: Expected to occur in certain circumstances.
* 5 — Almost Certain: Expected to occur frequently or repeatedly.

Impact Scale

* 1 — Insignificant: Minimal operational or information security impact.
* 2 — Minor: Limited disruption or damage.
* 3 — Moderate: Noticeable disruption requiring corrective action.
* 4 — Major: Significant operational, financial, or reputational consequences.
* 5 — Severe: Critical disruption or substantial damage to the organization.

Risk Calculation

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

Risk Classification

* 1–4: Low
* 5–9: Moderate
* 10–16: High
* 17–25: Critical

The same scoring approach is used for initial and residual risk assessments.

5. Information Security Principles

The assessment considers the CIA Triad:

Confidentiality

Ensuring that information is accessible only to authorized individuals and systems.

Integrity

Ensuring that information remains accurate, complete, and protected against unauthorized modification.

Availability

Ensuring that information and systems remain accessible when required by authorized users.

These principles help evaluate the potential consequences of information security risks.

7. Assumptions and Limitations

The following assumptions apply to this project:

1. Nexora Solutions Pvt. Ltd. is a fictional organization created for educational purposes.
2. The assessment uses illustrative organizational assets and risk scenarios.
3. Risk scores are based on the defined assessment methodology.
4. Proposed controls do not automatically indicate actual implementation.
5. Control effectiveness requires supporting evidence and validation.
6. Residual risk acceptance requires appropriate management authorization.
7. This project does not constitute an official ISO certification or compliance audit.

⸻

8. Documentation and Evidence Principles

All assessment documents should follow these principles:

* Maintain consistent risk identification numbers.
* Keep risk descriptions clear and specific.
* Document the justification for control applicability.
* Distinguish proposed controls from implemented controls.
* Maintain evidence supporting implementation claims.
* Record risk treatment and acceptance decisions.
* Review documents when significant changes occur.

A consistent methodology is essential for conducting meaningful information security risk assessments.

This document establishes the assessment approach and reference materials used throughout the GRC project.
