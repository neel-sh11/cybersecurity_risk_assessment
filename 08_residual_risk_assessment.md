2. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to evaluate the information security risks remaining after the proposed risk treatment measures have been implemented and their effectiveness has been verified.

Residual risk represents the level of risk that remains after considering the effectiveness of implemented security controls.

This assessment helps determine whether the remaining risks are acceptable or require additional treatment.

Important: All security controls documented in this assessment are currently proposed. No reduction in risk is assumed until implementation and effectiveness have been verified.

⸻

3. Assessment Methodology

Residual risk will be evaluated using the same methodology applied in the initial risk assessment.

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

Both likelihood and impact are measured on a scale of 1 to 5.

Risk Rating Classification

* Low (1–4): Generally acceptable with routine monitoring.
* Moderate (5–9): Requires periodic review and monitoring.
* High (10–16): Requires additional treatment or documented management review.
* Critical (17–25): Requires immediate attention and further risk treatment.

Residual likelihood and impact must be reassessed based on actual control implementation and effectiveness.

6. Residual Risk Acceptance

Residual risks must be reviewed by the relevant risk owners and authorized management representatives.

The following acceptance principles will apply:

* Low Risk: May be accepted with routine monitoring.
* Moderate Risk: Requires documented review and periodic monitoring.
* High Risk: Requires additional treatment or formal management approval.
* Critical Risk: Requires immediate attention and further risk reduction.

Risk acceptance decisions must be documented, justified, and approved by the appropriate management authority.

7. Monitoring and Review

Residual risks should be reviewed periodically because threats, vulnerabilities, and organizational requirements may change.

Nexora Solutions Pvt. Ltd. should:

1. Conduct periodic risk reassessments.
2. Review the effectiveness of implemented security controls.
3. Monitor emerging threats and technical vulnerabilities.
4. Update the risk register when significant changes occur.
5. Reassess risks following security incidents or infrastructure changes.
6. Maintain evidence supporting control effectiveness.
7. Document changes in risk treatment and acceptance decisions.
