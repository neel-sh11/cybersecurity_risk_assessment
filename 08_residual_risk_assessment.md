2. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to evaluate the risks remaining after applying the proposed security controls and risk treatment measures.

Residual risk represents the level of risk that remains after security controls have been implemented.

The assessment helps determine whether the remaining risks are acceptable or require additional treatment.

⸻

3. Assessment Methodology

Residual risk is evaluated using the same methodology applied in the initial risk assessment.

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

Both likelihood and impact are measured on a scale of 1 to 5.

Risk Rating Classification

* Low (1–4): Generally acceptable with routine monitoring.
* Moderate (5–9): Requires periodic review and monitoring.
* High (10–16): Requires additional treatment or formal management approval.
* Critical (17–25): Requires immediate attention and further risk treatment.

Residual scores must be based on reassessed likelihood and impact, considering the actual effectiveness of implemented controls.

5. Initial Risk vs. Residual Risk

The effectiveness of risk treatment should be evaluated by comparing the initial risk scores with the residual risk scores.

The following calculation is used:

Risk Reduction = Initial Risk Score − Residual Risk Score

A positive result indicates a reduction in the assessed risk level.

The comparison will be completed after validating the effectiveness of the implemented controls.

⸻

6. Residual Risk Acceptance

Residual risks must be reviewed by the relevant risk owners and management representatives.

The following acceptance principles will be applied:

* Low Risk: May be accepted with routine monitoring.
* Moderate Risk: Requires documented review and periodic monitoring.
* High Risk: Requires additional treatment or formal management approval.
* Critical Risk: Requires immediate attention and further risk reduction.

Risk acceptance decisions must be documented and authorized by the appropriate management authority.

⸻

7. Monitoring and Review

Residual risks should be continuously monitored because the threat environment and organizational infrastructure may change.

Nexora Solutions Pvt. Ltd. should:

1. Conduct periodic risk reassessments.
2. Review the effectiveness of implemented security controls.
3. Monitor emerging threats and technical vulnerabilities.
4. Update the risk register when significant changes occur.
5. Reassess risks following security incidents or infrastructure changes.
6. Document any changes in residual risk acceptance decisions.

⸻

8. Conclusion

The residual risk assessment provides an overview of the remaining information security risks after applying risk treatment measures.

Security controls can reduce the likelihood and impact of threats, but they cannot guarantee complete risk elimination.

Nexora Solutions Pvt. Ltd. should maintain continuous monitoring, periodic reassessment, and documented risk acceptance to support the effectiveness of its Information Security Management System (ISMS).
