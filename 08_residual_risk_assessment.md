1. Objective

The purpose of this document is to evaluate the cybersecurity risks that remain after implementing the proposed security controls and risk treatment measures.

Residual risk represents the level of risk remaining after security controls have been implemented and their effectiveness has been evaluated.

This assessment helps determine whether remaining risks are acceptable or require additional treatment.

The assessment uses the seven initial risks documented in File 04 and the proposed security controls documented in Files 05, 06 and 07.

2. Residual Risk Assessment Methodology

Residual risk will be evaluated using the same qualitative 5×5 risk assessment methodology applied in the initial risk assessment.

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

Both likelihood and impact are measured on a scale of 1 to 5.

2.1 Risk Rating Classification

* Low (1–4): Generally acceptable with routine monitoring.
* Moderate (5–9): Requires periodic review and monitoring.
* High (10–16): Requires additional treatment or formal management approval.
* Critical (17–25): Requires immediate attention and further risk reduction.

2.2 Assessment Requirements

Residual risk ratings must be based on:

1. Actual control implementation.
2. Supporting implementation evidence.
3. Control effectiveness testing.
4. Updated threat and vulnerability information.
5. Changes in likelihood and impact.
6. Risk owner and management review.

A proposed security control must not be treated as an implemented control.

Residual risk scores must remain pending until sufficient implementation and effectiveness evidence is available.

5. Initial Risk vs. Residual Risk

The effectiveness of risk treatment will be evaluated by comparing initial risk scores with reassessed residual risk scores.

The following calculation will be used:

Risk Reduction = Initial Risk Score − Residual Risk Score

A positive result indicates a reduction in the assessed risk score.

A zero result indicates no change.

A negative result indicates an increase in the assessed risk score.

6. Residual Risk Acceptance

Residual risks must be reviewed by the relevant risk owners and authorised management representatives.

The following acceptance principles will apply:

Low Risk

May be accepted with routine monitoring and documented review.

Moderate Risk

Requires periodic monitoring and documented management review.

High Risk

Requires additional treatment or formal management approval.

Critical Risk

Requires immediate attention and further risk reduction before acceptance can be considered.

All risk acceptance decisions must be documented and authorised by the appropriate management authority.

Risk acceptance does not remove the requirement for ongoing monitoring.

7. Monitoring and Review

Residual risks should be periodically reassessed because the threat environment, organisational infrastructure and business requirements may change.

Nexora Solutions should:

1. Conduct periodic risk reassessments.
2. Review the effectiveness of implemented security controls.
3. Monitor emerging threats and technical vulnerabilities.
4. Update the risk register when significant changes occur.
5. Reassess risks following security incidents or infrastructure changes.
6. Document changes in residual risk ratings.
7. Obtain management approval for formally accepted residual risks.
8. Maintain supporting evidence for all control effectiveness assessments.
